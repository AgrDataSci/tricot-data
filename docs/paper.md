# Abstract

We present the largest open dataset of decentralised on-farm agricultural experiments, produced with the tricot approach (triadic comparisons of technology options) by 28 research organisations across 22 countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America. The release holds 600 trial files covering 44 crops, 102,539 farmer-managed sites and 3.33 million plot-level observations, of which 2.45 million are rankings and 0.50 million are measurements, collected between 2012 and 2026. Each trial is published as one self-describing JSON file that carries its design, its sites with rounded coordinates, its observations, its genotypes and its full provenance, with producer, contributors and funder identified through persistent identifiers. Trait names are harmonised across trials and mapped to the Crop Ontology where terms exist. Researcher-managed companion data from the same genotypes, 47 on-station and mother trials, are linked to the tricot trials they complement. The data are released under CC0 1.0 on GitHub and archived on Zenodo under a permanent concept DOI. The dataset supports research on farmer preferences, genotype by environment interaction, climate adaptation, variety release and seed systems, and it is built so that people and AI agents fetch, filter and reuse it without accounts or tooling.

**Keywords.** citizen science, on-farm testing, tricot approach, participatory variety evaluation, open data, FAIR data, crop improvement, seed systems

# Background and summary

For fifteen years the tricot approach has grown through partnership. National research institutes, CGIAR centres, NGOs, seed companies and universities in 22 countries ran on-farm trials with a shared method and the ClimMob platform (<https://climmob.net/>), and farmers ranked 44 crops on more than 100,000 farms. Each trial served its partner’s decision on variety release, seed system development or breeding priorities^(1–4). This release brings those trials together as one open resource for the breeding and seed system community and beyond.

The dataset is reusable at this scale because every trial is published as one self-describing file in a common schema, with the credit of the organisation that produced it. That design is what let 45 organisations, 28 of them data producers, release 553 farmer-managed trials and 102,539 sites under CC0, together with 47 researcher-managed companion trials on the same genotypes. Methods explains how trials collected in different formats and languages became one schema without losing provenance or exposing farmers. Data Records describes the files and their coverage. Usage Notes explains how people and AI agents fetch the data and how credit flows back to the producers.

The data come from the tricot approach, proposed in 2011 for large-scale, decentralised citizen science in agriculture^(5,6). Options from a larger pool are allocated to participants in connected incomplete blocks of three. A farmer receives three coded seed lots, grows them side by side under local management and reports which performed best and worst for each trait. Because the triads overlap, the partial rankings of many farmers form one comparison network, and the relative performance of every option is estimated with Plackett-Luce models although nobody evaluates the full set^(1,7,8). Tricot trials have shown that these distributed observations support inference across environments¹, predict across-season performance better than station-trained genomic models⁹, extend to consumer tests and product concepts^(10,11), and aggregate into reliable estimates although each farmer’s ranking is imperfect^(12,13). Each trial was designed, run and owned by the organisation that needed the answer, under its own project and funder, on successive versions of ClimMob or in partner files^(8,14). Bringing them together meant harmonising the formats, languages and conventions each partner chose for its own question, which is the work this release documents.

Three features follow from the design and set the dataset apart from the trial data breeding programmes usually release. It was built from the bottom up, so the trust of the producers is written into the files. Each trial carries its producer with a ROR identifier, its principal investigator, its contributors and its funder, and credit travels with the data wherever they go. It is wide rather than deep. One trial of 150 farms says little on its own, while several hundred trials on 44 crops across 22 countries let an analyst ask which varieties perform where, how preferences differ between women and men, how performance responds to climate and whether on-farm and on-station results agree. And it is FAIR in full¹⁵ for people and for AI agents alike. One index lists every trial with the path to fetch it, one JSON file per trial describes itself completely, and nothing requires an account, a token or a database client.

This release is the production version of the dataset. A pilot release in 2025 tested the format on 62 trials from six African countries, and the data described here replace it, so users should work with this release. It harmonises trials collected with every version of ClimMob since 2012 and partner files into one schema, one naming rule and one licence, and sets the standard for future publications of tricot data from ClimMob. It is also the first release that reaches beyond Africa, to India and Central America, where tricot began.

# Methods

## The tricot design

The unit of the design is the set of three technology options evaluated by one participant. The options are most often crop varieties and breeding lines, and sometimes food products, agronomic practices or product concepts. The pool usually holds 8 to 12 comparable options and never fewer than two. In this release the median trial compares 10 genotypes and the largest 48. Each participant receives one incomplete block of three, which keeps the entry threshold low in land, time and training. Blocks are allocated at random by ClimMob in a sequentially balanced way, so that each option appears at roughly equal frequency and every village receives a balanced set even when distribution stops early^(6,14). Packages are coded A, B and C and the identity of the options is revealed only after the data are collected, to prevent bias toward familiar names.

Participants grow the three options at the same time in adjacent plots of equal size, under their own management, which maximises external validity. At defined crop stages they record on pictorial observation cards which option is best and which is worst for each trait, for instance germination, vigour, pest and disease resistance, drought tolerance, yield, taste and marketability, and for overall preference. Many trials also compare each option with the farmer’s own local variety, and a growing share records measured traits such as plot yield or days to maturity. Field agents, usually extension officers, digitise the cards with the ODK Collect app and submit them to ClimMob, where the project owner keeps full rights over the data¹⁴. Ranks are stored as the position of each option within the site, 1 being best, and the middle rank is inferred from the best and worst answers. Rankings are analysed with Plackett-Luce models, which estimate for each option the probability of outranking the others^(7,16), and with model-based recursive partitioning to find the climatic, geographic or socio-economic covariates that change the ranking^(1,17).

## Sources of the data

The release combines four kinds of sources (Table 1). ClimMob projects were exported with their project metadata, registration survey, data collection forms and package allocation, or recovered from curated spreadsheets prepared for earlier publications^(1,2). Partner files cover trials run with the tricot design outside ClimMob, among them the national on-farm trials of Integrated Seed Sector Development Ethiopia, the NextGen Cassava trials in Nigeria and Tanzania and the consumer and concept tests run with video-based ranking tools^(11,18). Researcher-managed companion data come from breeding databases and spreadsheets of the same programmes, 13 on-station breeding trials of groundnut in Tanzania and 34 participatory varietal selection mother trials of bread wheat and rice in India, which evaluate the same genotypes under researcher control.

## From source to release file

Two objections meet any pooled on-farm dataset. The trials are not comparable, and the farmers’ data are not protected. The pipeline answers both first, then takes care of identity, direction, credit, companion data and review. It runs as one command, kept under version control in the private working repository of the data managers, and rebuilds every release file from its source, so nothing in the published files is edited by hand. Each step has a decision record that states the question, the method, the alternatives rejected and what the result does and does not support. The records are published with the data so that a reader, or an AI agent, can judge whether a choice fits their own question.

**Harmonisation of traits and variables.** Every question asked in a trial was mapped to a canonical question in a harmonised question library of 414 entries that the protocols team maintains for all tricot crops¹⁹. The published trait name is the cleaned canonical code, so a yield ranking from Nicaragua in 2014 and one from Rwanda in 2024 share one name and one identifier. The label as asked is preserved in `trait_original`, and the identifier in `trait_id`, so every harmonised name traces back to its source. Where a Crop Ontology term exists for the crop and trait, its identifier is attached to the variable definition²⁰. Labels with no canonical match keep an English name and an empty identifier, and are listed as open questions for the producer.

**Standard names, units and places.** A harmonised code is still not one name per concept, since the same trait was asked under several codes, in several languages and in several units. One of us (R.J.H.) reviewed every trait, site variable, collection moment, unit, place name and categorical answer of the dataset and built lookup tables that the pipeline now applies. Traits and site variables take one English name per concept, and the name before the change is kept in `trait_original` and in the variable definition. Each collection moment keeps its label and gains a standard stage. Measured values keep the value and unit as recorded and gain the same value in one unit per trait. Yield per area is separated from the weight harvested from a plot, and a yield computed from a plot weight is flagged as derived, with a second flag when the plot area is the protocol size and not a reported one. Place names move to the administrative levels defined for each country, in GADM 4.1 spelling where they match, with the village in a separate column. Categorical answers of the site table have one spelling, in English. Site dates are changed only when they are clear typing errors and the other sites of the trial leave exactly one possible correction, which changed 25 dates and left 39 suspect dates listed for the producers.

**Personal data.** Names, relatives’ names, phone numbers, device identifiers, photographs, enumerator identities and free text are never exported. Administrative names and local variety names are kept. Gender and age are published as categories. Coordinates are checked against the national boundary, and points outside the country, with swapped axes or a flipped sign are removed and flagged. Every site coordinate is then rounded to two decimals, which places the published point within about 800 m of the recorded one and gives the same point on every run. Where the recorded point lies more than 3 km from the point published in the 2025 pilot release, because the form was filled away from the farm, the pilot point is kept and rounded. Sites without a coordinate take the mean of other located sites in the same village or district before rounding, with the source and the spread of the pool recorded in `geo_source` and `geo_uncertainty`. In this release 88,323 sites carry a point and 15,029 do not, the latter mostly consumer and concept tests and early India trials that recorded no village name.

**Identifiers.** The trial identifier is the only identity of a trial. Current ClimMob projects keep their 12-character hexadecimal project id. Older trials receive the first 11 hexadecimal characters of the MD5 hash of the country code and the original project code, followed by the letter v. The id has the same length and alphabet as a current id, cannot collide with one and reproduces itself on every run, which is how duplicates across sources were found. Sites are identified as trial id plus package number, and plots as the package letter A, B or C.

**Direction of rankings.** Some legacy forms asked which option was worst in the first column and best in the second, and some traits, such as faster maturity, can be valued in either direction. A check compares, at every site, the option ranked best for a trait with the option ranked best overall. Traits farmers value agree with the overall choice well above chance, and the median agreement of a trait across all trials describes how it normally behaves. A trait that agrees elsewhere but, in one trial, is the overall worst on more than half of at least 40 sites is reversed and every affected row is flagged `direction_inverted`. Ten trait and trial pairs were reversed. The same check exposed three bread wheat trials from India in 2014 whose answers are shifted across columns and cannot be repaired without the paper forms. They are published with a `consistency_warning` in their metadata.

**Producers, contributors and funders.** Where the source recorded no producer, the principal investigator of the ClimMob project and the project code decide, with the confidence and the evidence recorded in a reviewed table. An inferred producer carries an `attribution` note in the file, so a reader tells it from a stated one. Organisations were resolved against the Research Organization Registry; 21 of the 28 producers have a ROR identifier. Contributors follow the DataCite contributor types, with the authors of the publication that first used the data as project members, the person who curated the data as data curator and the data managers as such. Funders carry the award title and number where known. 460 trials name their funder, the largest being the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Netherlands (214 trials), BMZ (100), the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (92), the McKnight Foundation (79), the Gates Foundation (63), the CGIAR Research Program on Climate Change, Agriculture and Food Security (40), the Global Environment Facility (22), Norad (21), SDC (21) and USAID (21).

**Companion data.** Researcher-managed trials on the same genotypes are published in the same schema with `pvs` or `station` in the file name and `management = researcher-managed` in the index. Each companion file lists in `metadata.companion_of` the tricot trials it relates to, and each tricot file lists in `metadata.companions` the companion files that carry data on its genotypes, with the shared genotype names. Measurements made on genotypes outside the trial design, such as laboratory assays of a food product, go to a `genotype_data` table in the tricot file itself. The three tables share the same observation columns, so an analyst joins them on `genotype_name` to fit models with item covariates.

**Review before release.** The processed files were reviewed as a whole before this release. Variable names were cleaned of question identifiers, genotype names of crop prefixes, spellings merged within trials, taxa given one spelling per crop and placeholders set to null. Every open question that the data could answer was answered and marked as decided, 176 proposed answers were sent to the producers for confirmation and 104 questions remain open, mostly release years of varieties, author lists, plot sizes and units. Thirty-seven further trials with 7,409 sites were processed but held back pending the approval of their producers. They will enter the next version.

## Standard operating procedures

Most trials in this release predate the first published collection of tricot standard operating procedures, released in 2026 with crop-specific protocols for 20 crops, an abbreviated sweetpotato protocol and the core question library¹⁹. The harmonised question library used here is the same one those protocols are built on, so trials run under the SOPs from 2026 onwards enter the dataset without further mapping. About 80 percent of current ClimMob trials already follow an SOP, and the data structure described here is what those SOPs produce. In both directions the protocols and the data share trait definitions, identifiers and Crop Ontology terms, which is what lets on-farm rankings sit beside on-station measurements in breeding systems^(20,21).

# Data Records

The release is published on GitHub at AgrDataSci/tricot-data and archived on Zenodo under the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17112491²². The concept DOI always resolves to the latest version. The version DOI of this release, \[10.5281/zenodo.XX\], identifies the exact files described in this Data Descriptor. The GitHub release is tagged with the same version, and the Zenodo record holds the full data, never only a link. All files are released under CC0 1.0.

**Layout.** `datasets.csv` at the repository root is the finding aid, one row per trial with the trial id, crop and taxon, country, data producer, start and end dates, trial type and management, objective, numbers of participants, sites, genotypes and observation rows, the collection moments, the ranked traits, the size in megabytes, the licence and the path to fetch. Trial files sit in `data/<crop>/` and are named `<crop>-[kind-]<iso3>-<trial_id>.json`, where `kind` is `pvs` or `station` for companion data. Crop, country and year do not identify a trial, 313 of the 366 legacy files share all three with another file, so the trial id closes every name and no user composes a file name by hand. `metadata/` holds the JSON Schema, the data dictionary and the DataCite metadata of the release, `decisions/` the decision records of the pipeline, and `docs/` the map and this paper. The release is 1.0 GB of minified JSON. A single trial file is read with any JSON parser, and a user fetches the five trials they need, not the whole set.

**File structure.** Every trial file follows the same release schema with up to four parts (Table 2). `metadata` describes the trial, `site_data` has one row per site, `plot_data` is the long table of observations and `genotype_data` holds measurements on genotypes outside the trial design. The files hold observations only; model estimates such as Plackett-Luce log-worths are left to the analyses that use them. Site and plot rows link through `site_id`, genotype rows through `genotype_name`, and `metadata.variables` lists and describes every column of the three tables with its harmonised question id, Crop Ontology term, value type and unit. Every file states its schema version, the version and date of the release, the export script and interpreter that produced it, the concept DOI and the licence, so a file found anywhere identifies the dataset it belongs to.

**Coverage.** The dataset holds 600 trial files from 22 countries and 44 crops, produced by 28 organisations (Figure 1, Table 3, Supplementary Table S1). The 553 farmer-managed tricot trials comprise 540 on-farm variety trials, 8 consumer tests and 5 concept tests, with 102,539 sites, each the farm or household of one participant, and 3.23 million observation rows. Two cassava files from Nigeria hold one field trial in which the woman and the man of each household were interviewed separately, and their study descriptions say so. The 47 researcher-managed companion trials add 513 station and mother-trial sites. Across all files `plot_data` holds 3,330,662 rows, of which 2,476,635 are rankings, 508,306 numeric measurements, 100,445 categorical answers, 100,824 dates and 91,965 comparisons with the local variety, and `genotype_data` holds 7,200 rows. Participants were 31,438 women, 41,658 men and 29,743 whose gender was not recorded. Participant identifiers are specific to each trial, so counts summed across trials are not counts of unique people. The trials cover 6,984 genotype entries, 401 standard trait names and 414 harmonised question identifiers, 41 of them mapped to Crop Ontology terms. Ethiopia (227 trials, 24,887 sites), India (114 trials, 22,950 sites) and Tanzania (59 trials, 15,124 sites) hold the most sites, and common bean, bread wheat, maize, groundnut and rice the most among crops. Trials started every year from 2012 to 2026. The dataset grew in two waves, the pilots and national programmes of 2013 to 2014 and the ClimMob expansion of 2017 to 2019, and added between 6,600 and 8,800 sites per year from 2021 to 2024 (Figure 2).

# Technical Validation

Each step of the pipeline carries its own check, with counts, in the decision records published with the data. The checks below are the ones that hold for the release as a whole.

**Design integrity.** Every tricot site holds exactly three plots of distinct genotypes from the trial pool, and every ranking is a complete order of those three. Ties, incomplete answers and package codes used by two participants with different answers were dropped at export and counted in the export reports, so no ranking in the release is ambiguous. Legacy trials with fewer than five ranked sites were not published (391 early ClimMob projects holding 30 ranked records in all), nor were 17 Ethiopian projects shown to be copies of another project, matched package by package.

**Consistency.** After the ten documented reversals the direction check returns no further candidate on any trait of any trial. The three inconsistent India wheat trials stay in the release with a machine-readable warning rather than being dropped, because their site, management and yield data remain sound.

**Identity and privacy.** No two files map to the same path, and every file carries the same trial id in its name, its metadata and the index. Every published point lies inside its country. No value in `site_data` matches a phone number or email pattern, and no published column refers to names, phones, device identifiers or photographs.

**Schema validation.** Every file is validated against the JSON Schema of the release before it is tagged \[XX, confirm once the schema is written and the validation run\]. The index is rebuilt from the files, so its counts agree with the files by construction.

**Reproducibility.** The whole release is rebuilt by one command from the sources, and each file records the script and interpreter version that wrote it. Independent reanalysis of the published files reproduces results of earlier studies built on subsets of the same data \[XX, cite the reproduction in tricot-analysis-workflows once run, e.g. Brown et al. 2022 for the Central American beans\].

# Usage Notes

**Finding and fetching.** Start from `datasets.csv`. Filter by crop, country, year, type, management, trait or producer, then fetch the files in the `path` column from the GitHub raw URL or from the Zenodo archive. A person, a script or an AI agent needs one URL per file and no login. The repository ships a skill file (`SKILL.md`), an `AGENTS.md` and an `llms.txt` that tell an AI assistant what the data hold, how to fetch them and how to cite them. The README gives a worked example in R that reads a trial file, builds the rankings with the gosset package and fits a Plackett-Luce model with PlackettLuce^(7,16); the ClimMobTools package reads the same structure. The companion tricot-analysis-workflows repository holds the team’s curated workflows, from variety recommendation and genotype by environment analysis to the integration of trial sites with climate and soil data.

**Pooling trials.** The standard trait names, units and identifiers make pooling straightforward, but the analyst decides what to pool. Trait wording, collection moments and local varieties differ among trials, and `trait_original`, `collection_moment` and the variable descriptions carry what is needed to judge comparability. Trials with a `consistency_warning` should be read before use in trait by trait analysis. Rankings flagged `direction_inverted` were corrected and the flag is informational.

**Covariates.** Rounded coordinates are fit for linking sites to gridded climate, soil and elevation data at kilometre resolution^(1,2,23), and the climatrends and chirps R packages were written for that purpose. They are not farm locations. Planting dates let an analyst compute season-specific covariates, and the companion files let item covariates measured on station enter the model.

**Credit and citation.** CC0 places no legal obligation on users. The community norm the dataset asks for is the one GBIF (<https://www.gbif.org>) asks of its users. Cite the version DOI you used and name the concept DOI so others find the current one. Cite, alongside the dataset, the publications that first used the trials you reused, which are listed in each file’s metadata, and list the trial ids you used in your paper or supplement. Author lists grow with versions, since each Zenodo version carries its own creators, so producers added in a later batch appear from that version on.

**Governance.** Producers approve the release of their trials, and 37 processed trials wait in quarantine for that approval. Personally identifiable information never leaves the ClimMob project owner’s control. Local variety names are published because they are part of the agricultural knowledge the trials record, and the dataset makes no claim on the traditional knowledge they carry. The dataset is maintained by two data managers in the margins of other work, which is why the design puts everything into self-describing files and one index, with no database or API to keep alive.

**What comes next.** Version 3 will add the quarantined trials, fuller Crop Ontology coverage and the trials run under the published SOPs. Trials collected through speech-based interfaces and trials that link to station data through breeding information systems will enter the same files, since the schema already carries companion links and measured traits. The dataset is one layer of a documented ecosystem that also publishes the protocols that produce the data, the evidence built on them and the analysis workflows that use them, each with its own identifier, so that a user can move from a question to the trials behind the answer.
